## Peer-reviewed publication

### Economic Analysis of Falls in a Private Hospital in Southern Brazil—A Case–Control Study

**Victor Antoniazi Gonzalez (first author)** and colleagues. *International Journal of Nursing Practice*. **2024;30(6):e13313.** First published online October 21, 2024.

- **Article:** https://doi.org/10.1111/ijn.13313
- **PubMed:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39431424/
- **ORCID:** https://orcid.org/0000-0001-7577-464X
- **Journal / publisher:** *International Journal of Nursing Practice*, Wiley

**Research question.** How do hospital costs differ between hospitalized patients who experience falls and those who do not, accounting for relevant clinical characteristics?

**Study design.** A matched case-control analysis using hospital data from January 2020 to December 2022, with a faller-to-nonfaller ratio of 1:2.

**Published finding.** The study reported median costs of **US$7,520.26** for patients who experienced falls compared with **US$6,144.24** for patients without falls (**p < 0.01**). The interpretation supports fall-prevention measures as a patient-safety and economic priority. These are findings of the published research, not software performance metrics.

**Why it matters to my engineering work.** The project reinforced the importance of precise data definitions, statistical reasoning, transparent limitations and clear communication of complex findings. Those skills are relevant when working with healthtech, administrative datasets, operational analytics and data-heavy systems.
